# Two-Factor Authentication (2FA / MFA)

## 1. What I Learned
Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) adds a second layer of identity verification beyond just a password. This second factor can be something like an authenticator app code, SMS OTP, email code, or hardware key.

It helps reduce account compromise even if the password is exposed.

## 2. Why It Matters
Passwords alone are often not enough because they can be leaked, guessed, reused, or phished. 2FA significantly improves account protection by requiring an additional proof of identity.

This is especially important for high-value accounts like email, GitHub, and banking-related services.

## 3. Practical Exploration
I reviewed whether 2FA was enabled on important accounts and observed what type of second factor was being used.

I also enabled 2FA on one important account and understood the role of backup/recovery codes.

## 4. Key Takeaway
- 2FA protects accounts even if passwords fail
- Not all 2FA methods are equally strong
- Backup and recovery planning are part of proper account security

## 5. Tools / Concepts Used
- 2FA / MFA
- Authenticator app awareness
- Backup codes
- Account recovery planning
